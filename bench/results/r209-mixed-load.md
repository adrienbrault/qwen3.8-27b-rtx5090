# Mixed prefill+decode at concurrency 8 (2026-09-07/08, results `2026-09-07-r209-mixed-load/`, `-r209b-mixed-deep/`, `-r209c-mixed-8k/`, `2026-09-08-r209d-overlap/`)

[← all results](../RESULTS.md)

The decode rows above use short prompts, so they are close to pure decode, and a concurrent prefill row is a synchronized burst: every request prefills together, then every request decodes together. Neither is the serving regime, where chunked prefill batches an arriving request's prefill chunks alongside other requests' ongoing decode steps. These rows measure that sustained mixed state on the live server with `vllm bench serve --dataset-name random`, three arms: stock power limits, both cards capped at 400 W, then stock again as a control.

The regime is measured, from each request's phase timeline reconstructed on the client side. An earlier version of this section rested the claim on a 1 Hz sampler of `vllm:prompt_tokens_total` and `vllm:generation_tokens_total`, reporting `prefill_only` = 0.00 in every row. That check was structural and could not have failed: the prompt counter does not advance per prefill chunk but books the whole prompt at the step that emits a request's first token, an event that also advances the generation counter, so the sampler was tracking arrival rate. `vllm:iteration_tokens_total` derives from the same once-per-request record and is not an alternative.

The replacement uses only client-side data. `vllm bench serve --save-result --save-detailed` records per-request `start_times`, `ttfts` and `itls`, so request *i* is prefilling over `[start, start+ttft)` and decoding over `[start+ttft, +sum(itls))`; sampling those intervals on a 50 ms grid gives the number of requests in each phase at every instant. Measured from the eighth first token, where the eight concurrency slots are actually saturated, to the last arrival: 76.5% of wall time at 8K had prefill and decode in flight at once, with 23.5% decode-only and prefill-only genuinely 0.000, averaging 0.94 requests prefilling against 7.14 decoding. At 32K it is 91.5% both, 8.5% decode-only, 1.21 against 6.81. The engine was never idle. Because `--max-concurrency 8` sits below the `--max-num-seqs 16` cap, arrival-to-first-token is prefill compute rather than admission delay: `num_requests_waiting` was zero in 89% of 8K samples and 80% of 32K samples.

Baselines at stock limits. Input length is fixed within each row, so every arm does identical prefill work (384,000 input tokens per arm at 8K, 1,024,000 at 32K).

Two things follow from those numbers.

The cost of mixing is a stall, not a uniform slowdown, and a stall never happens without a prefill in flight. At 8K the median inter-token gap is 16.29 ms while the mean is 49.63 ms and p99 is 665 ms. Counting a stall as a gap above five times the row's median and classifying each gap by the number of prefills in flight at its midpoint: at 8K a gap stalls 26.2% of the time when a prefill is in flight (560 of 2,135) and 0.0% of the time when none is (0 of 7,045); at 32K, 44.1% (921 of 2,091) against 0 of 4,832. Stall size is one decode step in 16.4 at 8K, mean 485 ms and median 658 ms, and one in 7.5 at 32K, mean 854 ms and median 904 ms. A second reading needs no null at all: the share of a decoding stream's elapsed time spent stalled (66.2% at 8K, 87.4% at 32K) tracks the share of wall time with a prefill in flight (71.1% and 91.0%). The mechanism is one chunked-prefill step sharing that stream's batch (`--max-num-batched-tokens 8192`, shared with the running decodes, so a chunk is smaller than the full budget). A user sees output pause for half a second to a second when another request's prompt arrives, not slower tokens throughout.

Deep prefill holds at the median and pays at the tail. The 32K mixed TTFT p50 of 4.345 s is comparable to the isolated single-stream 30K TTFT of 4.305 s measured in the power section; the agreement should not be read to 1%, since that row prepends filler words to a nominal 30K target whose true token count was never measured, while these prompts are exactly 32,000 tokens. Its p99 is 23.4 s, and that tail is a start-up artefact rather than steady-state queueing — though the two rows reach it differently. At 8K the client starts its first 8 requests at the same instant (measured spread 0.001 s), so they prefill back to back: the p99 there is held by the sixth arrival, which started at t=0, and eight stacked ~1.1 s prefills reproduce the 7.1 s figure. At 32K arrivals are Poisson at 0.6/s rather than simultaneous, spreading the first eight over 16.2 s, so the tail is arrivals outpacing 4.3 s prefills until the concurrency cap fills; in a repeat run the p99 was held by the eighth arrival, landing at t=16.25 s just as every slot became busy. Once arrivals are gated by completions, about one request prefills at a time, which is why the median stays near the isolated figure. Read the p99 as a cold-start cost, not a warm-server one.

Mixed load is also the first regime other than isolated deep prefill to draw more than 400 W, so unlike decode, a 400 W cap does bind here. A repeat run read the 32K row at 429/388 W mean and 462/420 W p95, reproducing the table above; it read the 8K row lower, at 364/330 W mean and 379/345 W p95, so 8K sits at the 400 W floor and straddles it from run to run while 32K is clearly above. The cap remains inexpensive:

| Row | TTFT p50 | TTFT p99 | TPOT p50 | ITL p99 | Total tok/s | Draw mean | SM clock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8K, capped at 400 W | +4.5% | +4.7% | +2.4% | +4.6% | -4.4% | 334/309 W | 2,871 -> 2,753 MHz |
| 8K, control | +0.2% | -0.2% | +5.2% | -0.1% | +2.2% | 390/342 W | 2,879 MHz |
| 32K, capped at 400 W | +10.0% | +5.5% | +9.2% | +7.7% | -7.5% | 349/332 W | 2,856 -> 2,669 MHz |
| 32K, control | -0.1% | -8.6% | +3.6% | +0.2% | -4.4% | 434/387 W | 2,856 MHz |

The controls land at +0.2% and -0.1% on TTFT p50 and at -0.1% and +0.2% on ITL p99, so the capped arms' costs are real rather than noise. TPOT under the cap at 8K (+2.4%) sits inside its own control swing (+5.2%), which matches the decode result in the power section: capping does not touch decode, it slows prefill.

Two caveats on the absolute figures. Random-token prompts make the model emit low-entropy output that the MTP drafter predicts easily, giving an acceptance length of 3.45-3.72 out of a maximum 4 (86-93%), above what real code produces; decode here is therefore easier than production load and these throughputs should not be compared with the rows elsewhere in this file. Only the arm-to-arm deltas transfer, and they are sound because acceptance matched across arms. Random prompts also share no prefixes, making this the no-reuse worst case for prefill, whereas real agent traffic reuses 80-90% of its prefix; the cap costs less in practice than it does here.
