## Conditions

- **Configuration:** the served launcher (R231/R234: nvidia/Qwen3.8-27B-NVFP4, NVFP4 KV at the 14.86 GB pin, MTP at 3 draft tokens, `pcie_ipc` all-reduce, batch-sharded sampling), image `vllm-qwen38:v0290rc2-nvfp4kv-revival-prs-fi0616-pcieipc-bsshash-mtppcie-mtpcache-eagleshift`, started through its experiment path with the sequence limit at 64. Differences from the served engine: `--max-num-seqs 64`; `max_cudagraph_capture_size` capped at 320, because the MTP drafter on the `pcie_ipc` all-reduce refuses capture sizes above the 320 rows of its buffer ([R206c](r206c-mtp-c32-c64.md)); port 8029 bound to 127.0.0.1; the disk tier on a separate filesystem, emptied before every boot.
- **Boots:** 31 boots, one for the decode curve, 28 for the benchmark cells and 2 for the re-run. All had the same image id, engine arguments and environment, a 1,391,795-token pool (the served pool; the pool follows the pin, not the sequence limit), the same two compiled-graph artifacts (the first boot compiled them, every later boot loaded them), stock power limits of 600 / 575 W, core clock offset 0 and memory clock offset +4500, read back at boot and after each cell. Free VRAM after boot read 2,345 to 3,141 MiB per card.
- **Decode alone:** [`scripts/decode_ss.py`](../../scripts/decode_ss.py) on the first boot, one code and one prose prompt, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64 streams starting together, 1,024 forced tokens per stream, greedy, three runs per shape. The rate is taken over the samples where every stream was decoding, median of the three runs; acceptance is accepted draft tokens over draft tokens from the server's counters. Every run used a new prompt prefix; 0 cached prompt tokens.
- **Standard benchmark:** `vllm bench serve` v0.30.0 through the same request shim, samples and metrics as [R793](r793-27b-std-bench.md): ShareGPT V3, 400 conversations, seed 7310, outputs forced to the reference reply's length (mean 210 tokens, 4 to 1,439); Spec-Bench, 480 questions, 256 tokens each; greedy, streamed, thinking on; closed loop at 1, 4, 8, 16, 32, 48 and 64 concurrent requests; a fresh boot per cell; pass A over all 14 cells, then pass B; a cell is the mean of the two. The requests are R793's, in the same order, in every cell.
- **Metrics:** output tok/s is all completion tokens over the time from the first request's start to the last completion. Per stream is 1000 / TPOT p50, TPOT being (latency − time to first token) / (output tokens − 1) per request.

## 64 requests run together without preemption

- At 64 streams every `decode_ss` run had a window with all 64 requests decoding, and the engine logged 64 running in the decode boot and in 2 of the 4 cells at 64 streams (63 in the other two; the engine logs every 10 s). No request was preempted in the decode sweep or in any of the 30 cells.
- The engine's KV-cache gauge peaked at 80.9 % in the decode sweep (64 requests of about 1.1K tokens each) and at 79.6 to 81.0 % in the cells at 64 streams. 81 % of the 1,391,795-token pool is about 1.13M tokens for 64 requests; the remaining 19 %, about 265K tokens, is about 4.1K tokens of further context per request when all 64 run. The per-sequence linear-attention state and the reserved block take most of each request's share ([docs/DESIGN.md](../../docs/DESIGN.md#what-a-request-costs-in-the-pool)).

## ShareGPT at 16 streams comes from a re-run of that pair

- **The miss:** the round's rule, fixed before it ran, publishes a cell when passes A and B are within 3 % on output tok/s (1 stream exempt, as in R731 and R793). ShareGPT at 16 streams read 1,464.8 / 1,513.7 tok/s, 3.29 % apart; the other 13 cells were within 1.49 %. The round's decision line is `NOT-PUBLISHABLE 13/14 PUBLISHABLE; not: sharegpt c16 (A/B spread 3.29 % > 3 %); phase 1 OK`.
- **Pass A was the slow one.** In the per-token timings of the serving host's records (stripped from the published JSONs), pass A's decode frames take 17.2 ms (median) for the first 30 s of the 57 s run and 17.6 ms from then (about 20:56:38 UTC) to its end, and the frames longer than twice the median, which carry other requests' prefill, go from 39 to 48 ms. Pass B's stay at 17.1 and 39 ms in both parts, and both Spec-Bench cells at 16 streams are flat. No other cell up to 32 streams moves by more than 0.9 % between its halves. No GPU clock or power telemetry was recorded during cells, so the cause is not known. That boot came up on its first attempt.
- **Re-run and decision:** R794b re-ran the pair with the same driver, sample and launcher (two fresh boots, both on the first attempt, 2026-09-28 23:13 to 23:22 UTC). Its rule was written at 23:12 UTC, before its numbers existed: take its A/B mean if its spread is at most 3 % and the mean lies within 3 % of R794's pair mean of 1,489.2. R794b read 1,509.8 / 1,502.0 tok/s, spread 0.52 %, mean 1,505.9, 1.12 % above, so the cell is R794b's. The re-run's own decision line reads `NOT-PUBLISHABLE 1/1 PUBLISHABLE; phase 1 not run` only because it ran the benchmark phase alone; its one cell passed.
- **What this does not show:** the rule's band, 1,444.5 to 1,533.9, contains both of R794's passes, so passing it does not say which pass was off. R794b's two passes lie within 0.8 % of R794's pass B and 2.5 to 3.1 % above pass A, which agrees with the timing diagnosis; the rule did not test it.
- **What this changes:** the round's rule was applied per cell, as fixed before the run; this cell was measured in a later session than the other 13. R794's own mean of 1,489.2 would move the plotted point by 1.1 %.

## Above 32 streams the ShareGPT figure is bound by its tail

ShareGPT output tok/s rises 8.0 % from 32 to 48 streams and 0.9 % from 48 to 64; Spec-Bench rises 11.3 % and 2.0 %, and decode alone 13.6 % and 10.2 % (code). At 64 streams the 400 ShareGPT requests are 6.25 waves, and the outputs run from 4 to 1,439 tokens, so fewer than 64 requests were in flight for 16 to 17 % of the cell's wall time, against about 5 % for Spec-Bench's fixed 256-token outputs. The flattening therefore mixes what the server delivers with the sample's tail. It is not a measured ceiling of the server, and these records do not show whether a longer ShareGPT sample would keep rising above 48 streams.

## May not be claimed

- **Against the served 16-sequence figure** ([R675](r675-27b-curves.md), [R793](r793-27b-std-bench.md)): at 1 to 16 streams R794 reads higher, by 0.7 to 13.2 % on the standard benchmark (16 streams: 1,506 / 1,769 against 1,330 / 1,628 output tok/s, ShareGPT / Spec-Bench) and by 10.5 % (code) and 5.6 % (prose) on decode alone at 16 streams. The image, checkpoint, pin, pool, power limits and, for R793, the day and memory clock offset are the same; the sequence limit, the graph-capture list, the compiled artifact, the port, the disk tier and the session differ, and R675 did not record its memory clock offset. The two rounds are not an A/B, no cause is claimed, and the served figures remain those of the 16-sequence boot. A paired test would interleave boots at 16 and 64 sequences.
- **Against R206c** (2026-09-06, the seq-64 figure until this round): decode alone reads 2,699 / 3,838 / 4,807 t/s of code at 16 / 32 / 64 streams against 2,651 / 3,809 / 4,497, and 3,191 / 3,961 of prose at 32 / 64 against 3,022 / 3,608. R206c ran the RedHatAI checkpoint at the 13.98 GB pin (pool 1,309,368), did not record its memory clock offset, and its MTP head accepted 0.69 of code draft tokens against 0.64 to 0.66 here. The differences are not a gain of this configuration.
- **Solid against dashed:** as in R793, the gap between the lines is not a measure of what prefill costs decode; output length, content, interleaved prefill and turnover all differ between them.
- vLLM's `max_concurrent_requests` and `max_output_tokens_per_s` fields and the p99 columns of `summary.txt` are not quoted. The engine's maximum-waiting read-out spans the whole boot, the launcher's pre-warm included, so it is not quoted per cell either.
