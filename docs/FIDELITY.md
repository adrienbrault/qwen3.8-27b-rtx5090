# Fidelity against the bf16 model

Every checkpoint, KV dtype, state precision and draft length in this repo is ranked against the unquantized bf16 model, not against a task benchmark. The reason is in the first finding below: task benchmarks cannot see differences of the size that separate quantized checkpoints.

Two rulers, both teacher-forced on the served engine and scored against bf16 dumps of the same positions ([scripts/fidelity_ladder.py](../scripts/fidelity_ladder.py) makes the dumps, [scripts/fidelity_compare.py](../scripts/fidelity_compare.py) scores them):

- **Dense**: 693 documents, 555,549 scored positions of text and code. Reports top-1 agreement with bf16, corpus perplexity delta, and truncated KL over the bf16 top-k.
- **Agentic**: 72 tool-calling turns generated by the bf16 model, 57,972 positions, so the ruler scores the arm on the distribution an agent actually produces ([scripts/agentic_ref.py](../scripts/agentic_ref.py)).

A third ruler covers the decode path, which the two above cannot see: 20 greedy continuations of 80 chunks at 0 and 30K context, compared token by token with a bf16 decode reference ([scripts/decode_fidelity.py](../scripts/decode_fidelity.py), reference from `results/2026-09-04-r173c-bf16-decode`).

## What the rulers established

Nine NVFP4 checkpoints were compared on 725K dense positions and the agentic set on 2026-09-01 (`results/2026-09-01-r156-bf16-ladder`, decision record [R156-DECISION.md](R156-DECISION.md)).

- Task benchmarks cannot see differences of this size. GSM8K at n=250 resolves about 8 percentage points; the checkpoints differ by less than one, and by 4 points of top-1 agreement against bf16.
- The quantizer recipe matters more than the bit width. Three checkpoints at the same 4-bit width sit at +0.37% perplexity from bf16; one sits at +4.46%.
- A 4-bit `lm_head` alone costs about 0.85 percentage points of perplexity.
- fp8 KV costs +0.13 points of perplexity and does not grow with context out to 171K. 4-bit KV costs +0.76.
- Draft acceptance measures agreement between drafter and target, not quality. Re-run the drafter A/B after every checkpoint change.
- The draft length is a numerics change. Greedy continuations with 7 and 9 draft tokens diverge on 19 of 20 chunks at both 0 and 30K context, the same magnitude as fp8 versus nvfp4 KV, so a prefill-only ruler cannot rank draft lengths (`results/2026-09-04-r173b-ns-confirm`). 7 draft tokens read twice as far from the bf16 decode reference at 30K as 9 (0.0105 against 0.0052 median log-prob distance; R193d later found a per-boot draw of that size on one configuration, and R197 found that each draft length compiles its own artifact, so this reading does not rank the lengths on agreed tokens, `results/2026-09-04-r173c-bf16-decode`) and was retracted.
- Two boots of the same configuration are bit-identical on 725K positions and on 20 greedy decode chunks, so any difference between arms is the arm (`results/2026-09-04-r168e-splitkv-bf16`, `results/2026-09-04-r168d-splitkv-ref`). Two bf16 boots differ by 0.0002 on the decode ruler.

## The checkpoint

The served weights are [RedHatAI/Qwen3.8-27B-NVFP4](https://huggingface.co/RedHatAI/Qwen3.8-27B-NVFP4): W4A4 NVFP4 from llm-compressor, 303 modules kept at 8-bit, fp8 `lm_head`. [unsloth](https://huggingface.co/unsloth) and [kelnei](https://huggingface.co/kelnei) exports tie it on both rulers. The drafter is [syvai/Qwen3.8-27B-DFlash2-W4A16](https://huggingface.co/syvai/Qwen3.8-27B-DFlash2-W4A16). The gittensor checkpoint served before it cost 4.5 points of top-1 agreement on dense text and 3.4 on agentic turns, which no task benchmark in this repo detected: its GSM8K and tool-eval scores are within noise of the RedHatAI checkpoint's, and no SWE-Bench run was made on it ([R156-DECISION.md](R156-DECISION.md)).

## Comparing two engines: the determinism protocol (2026-09-05, R193 to R193d)

Two boots of one configuration do not produce the same logprobs by default. R193 (`results/2026-09-05-r193-determinism`) found that fresh torch.compile artifacts differ through the inductor reduction configs they bundle, and R193c and R193d (`results/2026-09-05-r193c-bss-determinism`, `results/2026-09-05-r193d-boot-lottery`) found a second draw taken at process start by the runtime Triton autotune of the GDN chunked-prefill kernels. Either draw moves the greedy decode ruler by about 0.0004 median at ctx 0 and 0.003 to 0.006 at 30K, and the dense ruler by about 0.035 % perplexity. A numerics comparison between two engines is therefore valid only when both load one compile artifact (equal compile-hash set in the boot log, the second boot loads and saves nothing) and both run with `VLLM_TRITON_FORCE_FIRST_CONFIG=1`. Under those two conditions a change that does nothing reads 20 of 20 chunks with median 0 at ctx 0 and at 30K, on three boots of one artifact (R193d's two, R193e's third; R193e also read the sharded sampler bitwise against them), and any divergence is the change. A bitwise pair across two modes is a valid null on its own, since two boots agree only if the draw coincided and the change did nothing; a divergent pair below one draw is not evidence of anything. The absolute distances to bf16 in the tables above carry both draws as noise; differences between candidates smaller than one draw are not differences. The knob costs nothing at decode and its prefill cost is unmeasured, so it is a ruler setting, not part of the served configuration.
